# 2015 - 2019

## 2015 - 2016 - Courses and the First Project

Decided to take it seriously and completed a three-month frontend course. Built my first project end
to end: build setup, linters, markup, scripts.

Started looking for a job. Without experience nobody hired me - rejections poured in even for a
modest salary. The main problem was the lack of commercial experience. I continued freelancing
alongside my regular job: making fixes and enhancements to existing landing pages and script logic.

## 2017 - 2019 - Email and Systematics

I put together a professional toolchain for producing email campaigns. Automation with gulp, image
compression, the mjml framework, linters, and automated validation of the output structure. It became
a ready-to-reuse component layer with complex responsive behavior inside the email structure. Emails
rendered correctly in all mail clients, and the result was automatically sent out for testing to all
types of email clients.

I kept freelancing in addition to my main job. This is where I first felt the power of engineering
in the web: deep study of the topic and full automation of the email pipeline gave me the confidence
that it's not about a university degree but about the approach to the work.

I studied JS concepts, systematic thinking, and framework structure in depth. I read about clean
architecture, OOP and functional programming principles, Mark Seemann on dependency inversion
principles, and Luis Atencio on functional programming. I watched Ben Lesh's conference talks on
RxJS. I got interested in Angular and React and discovered the magic of RxJS, Ramda, and Inversify.
I wrote a large number of pet projects - from Todo and RWC clones to Canvas experiments. I watched
some of the first videos by Tanner Linsley, soon after his React Query appeared.
