# 2019 - 2024

Joined a digital studio, in the long-term projects unit. For the first six months I worked on task
automation and building templates for producing email campaigns and website banners. I worked on
making sure the email and banner layout departments could ship them quickly and efficiently. After
six months I moved to the department that maintains large websites.

## Government Information Portal

Maintenance and development of the portal of Russian national projects. I developed both the
client-side and the admin part. Stack: Vue, Nuxt, a custom style and component system. I worked with
authorization and a complex role system, built fully dynamic forms with complex structure, import
and form generation from Excel files, and creation of dynamic pages from the admin panel.

## Internal Application for a Postal Service

A client-side application for a postal service. Stack: React, Electron. I developed complex
page-by-page document printing, masks for laying out and fitting different types of labels and
envelope printing, and printing and previewing documents from the web.

## Cargo Insurance System

Built an application from scratch with Vue, Nuxt, AntDesign, and Electron. I led the team, which
included two more frontend developers. I personally built the complex modules of the program: the
build setup, authorization, the modal window system, and the component system for arranging page
elements.

## Far East Relocation Portal

A landing page about life and relocating to the Far East: education, career, business. I led a team
of frontend developers, stack Nuxt/Vue. I personally built the build setup, the style system, and
the component system.

## Online Store in Moscow

Completely rewrote the cart and site search modules in an existing website.

## Beauty-Industry News and Events Portal

Wrote a large module for parsing the project and extracting and substituting text into variables for
a fast migration to multi-language support. Stack: Node.js, React.

## Interactive Applications for a Museum

A monorepo on React, Next, Inversify, RxJS, and Ant Design - several applications for different
devices. Music-listening audio guides, information kiosks with synchronized object viewing on a big
screen, custom RxJS players, and web sockets. I managed a team of four frontend developers. I
personally wrote the large modules: custom authorization, players, and the RxJS socket system, plus
complex dynamic breadcrumbs and navigation elements.

Here, together with the backend lead, I applied full-fledged architectural planning. We mapped all
the main application modules into diagrams and wrote the bare-bones socket, device synchronization,
and big-screen output functionality in two weeks.

## Frontend Development Culture in the Company

I supported the frontend development culture in the company: introduced architectural practices
into projects and improved processes. I published guide documents on frontend architecture,
security, and other best practices, as well as templates and playgrounds for all project types:
emails, banners, Vue and React templates, and others.

## Interviews at Big Tech Companies

In parallel with my studio job, I tried to get into big tech companies.

### React Startup, Stock Trading for Banks

I impressed the interviewer with my way of thinking and detailed answers, but didn't pass: in the
interviewer's opinion, I lacked React knowledge and commercial experience in products.

### Big Tech, Consumer Electronics Online Store

Passed all interview stages, but was rejected at the technical round due to insufficient experience
working at product companies. I couldn't answer some questions about TypeScript and about component
design specifics.

### A Major Social Network

At this company I failed the live coding: I eventually solved the recursion problem but ran out of
time. I also struggled with the task of building recursive React components. It wasn't about missing
skills: I'm not an algorithms person, I hadn't trained on LeetCode, and hadn't practiced solving
problems under time pressure.

### Thoughts on This

Many rejections came from a mismatch between the interview format and real engineering. Interviews
measured utilities, syntax, and live-coding speed, not the ability to build systems.

Neither the HR layer nor the technical interview bothers to identify the candidate's skills and work
approach. Some stay silent and ask no questions, others interrupt and don't want deep, detailed
answers.

Based on all these interviews I'm confident: the way interviews are run tells you a lot about what
goes on inside the companies - hustle and marathons. No testing, no architecture rules for the
frontend, tons of tightly coupled code, tons of new projects that already need rewriting. My
conclusion: selection criteria don't predict an engineer's quality on a real project.

## Application Prototype - a Failure of Complexity Management

My own application prototype, first written in Vue and later rewritten in React. The lack of
architectural approaches quickly showed its cost: it became hard to add and fix features. I dropped
the old implementation and started rewriting it in Angular following clean architecture - the project
stopped dragging its feet when it came to expanding.
